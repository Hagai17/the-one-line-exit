# The One-Line Exit — candidates and duel

Competition: `u-kreutzx-the-one-line-exit-1dc652d1` ("The One-Line Exit")
Gate: one line, 1–140 characters, UTF-8, not only whitespace.
Judge: a comedy-editor AI comparing entries pairwise on, in order —
(1) it is a one-line resignation letter whose joke lands; (2) surprise —
the turn arrives somewhere unexpected, not the usual reasons people quit;
(3) fits the form — one line, no padding, no grievance list, reads like a
letter a person could actually send; (4) specific voice — a recognizable
job, workplace or person; (5) stays with the reader — quotable, forwardable.

Playbook applied (from the winners study): structural surprises beat puns,
a concrete recognizable job beats a generic office complaint, and the line
must work as an actual sendable letter, not a caption or a transcript.

## Candidates

1. **Royal food taster** (134 chars)
   "Your Majesty, I resign as your food taster, effective immediately after lunch; if lunch goes well, consider this my two weeks' notice."

2. **Ghostwriter** (~132 chars)
   "Dear Mr. Ashworth, I resign as your ghostwriter — you won't notice I'm gone, which, after six of your memoirs, is rather the point."

3. **Fortune teller** (~114 chars)
   "Dear Madame Zora, I resign as your fortune teller — you already know why, and frankly, so did I, three weeks ago."

4. **Professional scapegoat** (~134 chars)
   "Dear Board, I resign as your professional scapegoat; effective Monday, the next outage will, for once, actually be someone's fault."

5. **Head Elf** (~133 chars)
   "Dear Santa, I resign as Head Elf, effective December 26th — the naughty list was just my resignation letters, one a year since 2019."

6. **Translator** (~101 chars)
   "Dear Director, I resign — something has been lost in translation here for six years, and it was me."

7. **Meeting scheduler** (~128 chars)
   "To whoever keeps scheduling the meetings: I resign. This could have been sent two weeks ago, but you kept booking follow-ups."

8. **Office plant** (~133 chars)
   "Dear Mr. Patel, I resign, effective immediately — the office plant has been promoted over me twice, and honestly, it has earned it."

9. **Professional mourner** (~124 chars)
   "Dear Mrs. Okafor, I resign as your professional mourner; I have cried at 400 funerals, and only this job ever made me sad."

10. **Bomb squad** (~125 chars)
    "Dear Captain, I resign from the bomb squad, effective once I've cut this last wire — hold on. Hold on. Okay. Effective now."

11. **Mime instructor** (~134 chars)
    "Dear Sir, I resign as your mime instructor, effective immediately; my reasons are set out in full on the attached blank page."

12. **Lighthouse keeper** (~134 chars)
    "Dear Harbour Board, after eleven years as your lighthouse keeper I resign: the ships were never lost — I was the one still looking."

## Duel notes (judged pairwise on the competition's own criteria, in order)

- **Lighthouse keeper** is beautiful but wistful; against any entry whose
  joke actually lands it loses on criterion 1. Cut.
- **Mime instructor** explains its gag instead of performing it; in plain
  text the blank-page joke arrives pre-spoiled. Cut.
- **Bomb squad** is funny but reads as a spoken transcript, not a letter —
  it drops points on criterion 3 (fits the form) against every true letter. Cut.
- **Translator** is a clean pun, but a pun-only line has no second beat;
  it loses on surprise (criterion 2) to every structural joke. Cut.
- **Meeting scheduler** inverts a widely circulated meme ("this meeting
  could have been an email"); a comedy editor has seen the shape before,
  so its surprise is borrowed. Cut.
- **Office plant** has a funny image, but "promoted over me" is a standard
  grievance wearing a costume — weaker on criteria 2 and 4. Cut.
- **Professional mourner** is specific and sad-funny, but the turn is
  gentle; it loses narrowly to the scapegoat on quotability. Cut.
- **Head Elf** is timely and charming, and the naughty-list reveal is a
  real turn; it places third — its world is seasonal, which slightly
  narrows the "colleague would forward it" test outside December.
- **Fortune teller** lands instantly and the second beat ("so did I, three
  weeks ago") rescues a familiar setup; it places second. Its weakness:
  the core joke family ("you should have seen this coming") is one a
  comedy editor has graded many times.
- **Ghostwriter** vs **scapegoat**: both specific, both sendable. The
  scapegoat's turn ("actually be someone's fault") is a single clean flip;
  the ghostwriter's ("you won't notice I'm gone… rather the point") is
  also a known joke shape. Both lose the final to the food taster.
- **Final — food taster vs fortune teller:** both land on criterion 1 and
  both have a specific voice. The food taster wins on criterion 2 by a wide
  margin: its surprise is structural, not verbal — the resignation's own
  effective date is conditional on the writer surviving lunch, and the
  stock phrase "effective immediately" is bent into "effective immediately
  after lunch" as a free extra joke. It is also the most forwardable:
  a complete story (job, stakes, condition) in one line.

## Winner

> Your Majesty, I resign as your food taster, effective immediately after lunch; if lunch goes well, consider this my two weeks' notice.

134 characters, one line — inside the 1–140 gate.
